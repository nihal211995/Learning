G1Search ranking v13: guest-fit features in the rankerCustomerSearch rankingStandard A/BShipAdvanced

Illustrative, hypothetical but realistic. Not real Airbnb data or results.

1. Context and Problem

Long result lists and low-fit listings near the top depress booking conversion. The ranking team proposes v13, which adds price-to-intent match and host-responsiveness features. Search drives most bookings, so a small relative lift is worth a large absolute volume, and a bad ranker is expensive and hard to attribute.

2. Hypothesis

H0: Booking conversion per searching user is equal under v12 and v13.
H1: v13 increases 7-day booking conversion per searching user; host-initiated cancellation rate does not rise.

3. Experiment Design

Unit of randomizationUser (logged-in plus stable device ID), not session or search: users search repeatedly and sessions are correlated. Listing-level interference (shared inventory) is real: control and treatment guests compete for the same listings. Bounded by a 50/50 user split plus a market-level cross-check on 3 holdout markets.
Control vs treatmentControl: ranker v12. Treatment: ranker v13 (same candidate set, re-scored).
Allocation / ramp1% dark launch for logging and latency check, 5% for 24h (SRM, latency), then 50/50 for the measurement window.
Eligibility / exclusionsAll users with a search with check-in in the next 365 days. Excluded: internal and bot traffic, users in the 2% global holdout, users in concurrent ranking experiments (mutually exclusive layer).

4. Metrics

Primary (OEC)7-day booking conversion per searching user = users with ≥ 1 booking within 7 days of first search ÷ users with ≥ 1 search.
SecondarySearch to listing-click rate (users with ≥ 1 click ÷ users); nights booked per searcher.
GuardrailHost-initiated cancellation rate per booking (≤ +0.25 pp tolerated); p95 search latency (+50 ms max).
DiagnosticRank position of booked listing; impression concentration across listings (Gini); share of new listings shown.

5. Sample Size and Power

Design inputsBaseline p₀ = 4.20%; MDE = 2.0% relative (0.084 pp absolute), p₁ = 4.284%; α = 0.05 two-sided, power = 80% (zα/2k = 1.960, zβ = 0.842)
VarianceVar per arm: p(1−p) → p₀(1−p₀)+p₁(1−p₁) = 0.08124
Formulan = (z1−α/2k + z1−β)² [p₀(1−p₀)+p₁(1−p₁)] / (p₁−p₀)²
Required n per arm903,698 users (planned 910,000 per arm; total 1,820,000 for two arms)
Duration640,000 eligible users/day across arms → 3 days of traffic → run 2 full weeks (whole weeks to cover day-of-week cycles). Traffic is sufficient in about 3 days; the 7-day conversion window and weekly cycle set the 2-week minimum.

Baseline from the trailing 8 weeks, same season. A 2% relative lift is about 0.084 pp: small, but material at search volume. CUPED on prior-30-day bookings is available if power is tight.

6. Pre-Launch Validation

  • SRM: expected split 50/50; observed 910,412 / 909,682; χ² = 0.29, p = 0.588 → pass.
  • A/A run on the same traffic layer for 7 days: no metric flagged at α = 0.05 beyond the expected 5%; variance estimate used in the power calculation.
  • Exposure logged at the first search impression; triggered and all-user analyses agree.
  • Seasonality: window avoids school-holiday weeks; day-of-week balanced by whole-week duration.

8. Evaluation and Interpretation

The primary lift of +0.11 pp (+2.6%) has CI [+0.05, +0.17] pp and p = 0.0002; the lower bound is above zero and above the 0.5% relative practical threshold agreed with Product. SRM passes (p = 0.59). The host cancellation guardrail is flat, and the upper end of its CI is inside the +0.25 pp tolerance. The new-vs-returning heterogeneity test is not significant (p = 0.3982), so one global effect is reported. Interference: the 3 market-level holdout markets show a same-signed lift, which bounds (not eliminates) the SUTVA concern; naive user-level estimates in supply-constrained markets can overstate the lift.

9. Decision and Rationale

Ship Ship v13 to 100%, keep a 5% v12 holdout for 8 weeks to measure the realized effect against the A/B estimate. Rationale: significant, practically meaningful, guardrails flat, consistent across tenure.

10. Key Learning

At search scale, significance is cheap: judge the CI lower bound against a practical threshold and check interference before extrapolating a user-level lift to the whole market.

10a. Lead Analyst Lens

  • Decision-quality trade-off: Wrong-ship cost is small (reversible, flat guardrails) while delay costs bookings every week, so the bar is a CI lower bound > 0 rather than a large lift.
  • Stakeholder message: Tell the PM: "+0.11 pp conversion is roughly +2.6% bookings; we expect the realized total effect to be somewhat smaller because of competition for inventory, and the holdout will tell us how much."
  • Follow-up / guardrail: Keep the 5% holdout and compare realized vs A/B-estimated lift (winner's-curse and interference calibration); alert on host cancellation drift.

G2Price display: nightly vs total vs total plus nightlyCustomerTotal vs nightly price; fee transparencyA/B/n multi-variantSegmentIntermediate

Illustrative, hypothetical but realistic. Not real Airbnb data or results.

1. Context and Problem

Guests compare nightly prices, then meet fees at checkout; this drives drop-off and fee-related support contacts. Product tests showing total price (incl. fees and taxes) in search results. Hypothesis: transparency lifts conversion and reduces fee complaints, but denser grids may suffer sticker shock.

2. Hypothesis

H0: Booking conversion is equal across nightly-price, total-price, and total-plus-nightly display.
H1: At least one total-price variant increases 7-day booking conversion; fee-related contacts fall.

3. Experiment Design

Unit of randomizationUser. Display is a per-user property and users should see a consistent price format across visits.
Control vs treatmentControl: nightly price. T1: total price (incl. fees, taxes). T2: total price with nightly price as secondary line.
Allocation / rampEqual thirds after a 3-day 5% ramp.
Eligibility / exclusionsAll users on search and map results in markets where fees are fully known at display time. Excluded: markets with variable tax rules that cannot be computed pre-checkout.

4. Metrics

Primary (OEC)7-day booking conversion per searching user.
SecondaryCheckout start rate; average booked total price.
GuardrailShare of bookings with a fee-related support contact (lower is better); host cancellation rate.
DiagnosticFilter-by-price usage; price-sort usage; scroll depth.

5. Sample Size and Power

Design inputsBaseline p₀ = 3.85%; MDE = 2.0% relative (0.077 pp absolute), p₁ = 3.927%; α = 0.05 two-sided, power = 80% (zα/2k = 2.241, zβ = 0.842)
VarianceVar per arm: p(1−p) → p₀(1−p₀)+p₁(1−p₁) = 0.07475
Formulan = (z1−α/2k + z1−β)² [p₀(1−p₀)+p₁(1−p₁)] / (p₁−p₀)²
AdjustmentsBonferroni/Holm for k = 2 comparisons: α/k = 0.025
Required n per arm1,198,280 users (planned 1,200,000 per arm; total 2,400,000 for two arms)
Duration520,000 eligible users/day across arms → 5 days of traffic → run 2 full weeks (whole weeks to cover day-of-week cycles). Three arms need 3 × the per-arm n; still about 7 days of traffic, so 2 full weeks.

Two comparisons against control, so α is split with Bonferroni/Holm: α/2 = 0.025 per test. Holm is used in the analysis, which is uniformly more powerful than Bonferroni.

6. Pre-Launch Validation

  • SRM: expected split 50/50; observed 1,200,280 / 1,199,850; χ² = 0.08, p = 0.781 → pass.
  • A/A on the 3-arm layer passed. Display logic unit-tested for tax and fee edge cases in 12 markets.
  • Seasonality: run covers a full week and avoids the peak-summer shift in lead time.
  • Pre-registered segments: device (app, mobile web, desktop web). No other cuts reported as findings.

8. Evaluation and Interpretation

After Holm correction both variants beat control, and T2 is the stronger (primary lift +0.12 pp, +3.1%, CI [+0.07, +0.17] pp). The aggregate hides a significant device interaction (Q-test p < 0.0001): app and mobile web gain, desktop loses. The fee-contact guardrail improves a lot, which is a real second benefit. Simpson-style risk: device mix is balanced across arms (SRM p = 0.78), so the pattern is not a mix artifact. The desktop loss is plausible (wider grids, total price appears larger next to peers) but is a hypothesis to test, not a result to explain away.

9. Decision and Rationale

Segment Ship T2 on app and mobile web. Iterate on desktop (layout variant with the nightly price in the primary slot) and re-test. Keep fee-contact reduction as a tracked benefit.

10. Key Learning

In an A/B/n, correct for multiplicity and then test heterogeneity: a positive pooled effect can hide a segment that you would harm by shipping uniformly.

10a. Lead Analyst Lens

  • Decision-quality trade-off: Shipping uniformly gains volume on 70% of traffic but loses on desktop; segment-level shipping costs little engineering and avoids a known negative.
  • Stakeholder message: Tell the PM: "T2 wins on app and mobile web; desktop is negative and we will not ship it there until the layout variant is tested."
  • Follow-up / guardrail: Pre-register the desktop follow-up with its own MDE; track fee-contact rate as a guardrail and benefit.

1. Context and Problem

Product believes guests who see a map first explore more and book better-fit listings. Map-first raises engagement (filters, panning), but the map tiles and pins add render time.

2. Hypothesis

3. Experiment Design

Unit of randomizationUser, because the default view should be stable across sessions.
Control vs treatmentControl: list-first (map toggle). Treatment: map-first (list in a drawer).
Allocation / ramp5% for 24h, then 50/50.
Eligibility / exclusionsDesktop web and tablet users with a search. Excluded: mobile app (different layout), users with a saved view preference.

Hypothesis testing, errors, power, p-value

H0: δ = 0 vs H1: δ ≠ 0  |  α = P(reject H0 | H0)  |  β = P(retain H0 | δ ≠ 0), power = 1 − β  |  p = P(|T| ≥ |tobs| | H0)

Symbols: δ true effect; α Type I rate; β Type II rate; T test statistic.

When to use: Every experiment: fix α, power and the decision rule before launch.

Confidence interval

CI95% = d̂ ± z0.975 · SE(d̂), z0.975 = 1.96 (t quantile for small df)

Symbols: d̂ estimated effect; SE its standard error.

When to use: Report with every estimate; judge the lower bound against the practical threshold.

Two-proportion z-test (pooled vs unpooled SE)

z = (p̂T − p̂C) / √[ p̂(1−p̂)(1/nT + 1/nC) ]   (pooled, for the test)
SEunpooled = √[ p̂T(1−p̂T)/nT + p̂C(1−p̂C)/nC ]   (for the CI)

Symbols: p̂ pooled conversion = (xT+xC)/(nT+nC).

When to use: Binary metrics (conversion, resolved / not resolved). Pooled under H0, unpooled for the interval.

Two-sample z / t and Welch's t-test

t = (x̄T − x̄C) / √(sT²/nT + sC²/nC)   df = (vT+vC)² / [ vT²/(nT−1) + vC²/(nC−1) ], v = s²/n

Symbols: s² sample variance. With large n, t ≈ z.

When to use: Continuous metrics with unequal variances (default over Student's t).

Absolute and relative lift, CI for lift

Δ = p̂T − p̂C   Rel = Δ / p̂C   CIRel ≈ CIΔ / p̂C (control treated as fixed); exact ratio CI: Fieller or delta method

Symbols: Δ absolute lift; Rel relative lift.

When to use: Always show both: leaders decide on absolute volume, scientists compare on relative lift.

Delta method for ratio metrics

R = x̄ / ȳ   Var(R) ≈ [ Var(x) + R²Var(y) − 2R·Cov(x,y) ] / (n · ȳ²)   SE(RT − RC) = √(Var RT + Var RC)

Symbols: x numerator per unit (contacts), y denominator per unit (bookings); n units.

When to use: Randomization unit ≠ analysis denominator (contacts per booking, revenue per booking).

Sample size for proportions

n = (z1−α/2 + z1−β)² [ p0(1−p0) + p1(1−p1) ] / (p1 − p0)²   per arm

Symbols: p0 baseline, p1 = p0(1+MDErel).

When to use: Binary OEC planning. Divide α by k for k comparisons; multiply by DEFF if clustered.

Sample size for means

n = 2σ² (z1−α/2 + z1−β)² / δ²   per arm

Symbols: σ outcome SD, δ absolute MDE.

When to use: Continuous OEC planning (handle time, revenue per user).

MDE from sample size

MDE = (z1−α/2 + z1−β) · σ · √(2/n)   (means)   MDE ≈ (z1−α/2 + z1−β) · √[2p(1−p)/n] (proportions)

Symbols: n per arm.

When to use: When traffic is fixed (small market, one queue) and you ask what lift is detectable.

Variance reduction: CUPED, stratification

θ = Cov(Y, X) / Var(X)   Yadj = Y − θ(X − X̄)   Var(Yadj) = Var(Y)(1 − ρ²)
Stratified: Var = Σs ws σs²/n (post-stratification re-weights by observed strata shares)

Symbols: X pre-experiment covariate, ρ corr(X,Y), ws stratum weight.

When to use: Sticky metrics with pre-period history (agent AHT, guest bookings). X must be pre-assignment.

Multiple testing: Bonferroni, Holm, Benjamini-Hochberg

Bonferroni: reject if pi ≤ α/m   Holm: sort p(1) ≤ … ≤ p(m), reject while p(i) ≤ α/(m−i+1)   BH: largest i with p(i) ≤ iα/m, reject 1..i

Symbols: m number of tests.

When to use: Bonferroni/Holm to control family-wise error (A/B/n arms); BH to control FDR (many segments or metrics).

SRM check (chi-square goodness of fit)

χ² = Σi (Oi − Ei)² / Ei, Ei = N · sharei, df = arms − 1; flag if p < 0.001

Symbols: O observed arm counts.

When to use: Before reading any result. Fails mean assignment or logging is broken; do not interpret the lift.

Sequential testing / always-valid inference

Group-sequential: stop at look k if |zk| > c·√(K/k) (O'Brien-Fleming-type; c = 2.024 for K = 4, α = 0.05)
mSPRT: Λn = √(σ²/(σ²+nτ²)) · exp[ n²τ² x̄² / (2σ²(σ²+nτ²)) ]; reject when Λn ≥ 1/α

Symbols: K planned looks; τ² mixing prior variance.

When to use: When stakeholders will peek, or when stopping early has value (harm, large win).

Bootstrap CI and permutation test

Bootstrap: resample each arm with replacement B times, d̂*b = x̄*T − x̄*C, CI = [q2.5, q97.5] of d̂*
Permutation: shuffle arm labels B times, p = #(|d̂*| ≥ |d̂|)/B

Symbols: B resamples (≥ 2,000).

When to use: Heavy-tailed or non-normal metrics (refund cost, handle time) and medians/percentiles.

Cluster-randomized design effect

DEFF = 1 + (m − 1) · ICC   neff = n / DEFF   SEcluster = SEnaive · √DEFF

Symbols: m average cluster size; ICC intra-cluster correlation.

When to use: Market/geo, agent, host-level or time-block randomization where units within a cluster are correlated.

Heterogeneous treatment effects

τ̂s = ȳT,s − ȳC,s   Interaction: Y = β0 + β1T + β2S + β3(T×S) + ε, test β3 = 0   Q = Σ ws(τ̂s − τ̄)², ws = 1/SEs² ~ χ²S−1

Symbols: S segment indicator; Q Cochran heterogeneity statistic.

When to use: Before shipping by segment: test the interaction, not each segment's p-value.

Difference-in-differences

τ̂DiD = (ȲT,post − ȲT,pre) − (ȲC,post − ȲC,pre)   Regression: Yit = α + β·Treatedi + γ·Postt + τ·(Treated×Post)it + ε, cluster SE by unit

Symbols: τ DiD effect. Needs parallel pre-trends.

When to use: Rollout by market/policy with no randomization; check pre-period event-study coefficients.

Synthetic control (brief)

ŵ = argminw≥0, Σw=1 Σt≤T0 (Y1t − Σj wjYjt)²   τ̂t = Y1t − Σj ŵjYjt (t > T0)   inference: RMSPE ratio vs placebo units

Symbols: Unit 1 treated, j donors, T0 pre-periods.

When to use: One or few treated units (one market, one queue) and a donor pool with good pre-period fit.

Bayesian A/B (Beta-Binomial)

pC ~ Beta(1 + xC, 1 + nC − xC), pT ~ Beta(1 + xT, 1 + nT − xT)   P(T>C) = P(pT > pC)   Expected loss(ship T) = E[max(pC − pT, 0)]

Symbols: Beta(1,1) flat prior.

When to use: Alternative framing for low-stakes, reversible calls; always pair with a loss threshold.

Surrogate: τ̂LT ≈ Σk βk τ̂Sk with βk from historical experiments linking short-term S to long-term Y

Interview Cheat Sheet

Lead-level questions with two-line answer frameworks. Open a question; each links to a case you can cite.

1. How do you choose an OEC for a two-sided marketplace feature?

Start from the decision and the long-term objective (nights booked that are completed and well-reviewed), pick one guest-side OEC, then pin host-side and quality guardrails with explicit thresholds before launch. Reject metrics that are easy to game or move without value.

Related cases: G5

2. Guest conversion is up, host cancellations are up. Ship?

Convert both to one unit of value (net completed bookings, plus cost of a host cancel: guest rebooking, support contacts, trust loss). If net is positive, ship with a mitigation arm; if not, iterate. Show the trade-off, do not pick a side silently.

Related cases: G5 H6

3. How do you handle interference in a marketplace test?

Name the channel (shared inventory, shared queue, price competition). Mitigate by cluster/market randomization, switchback, or buyer-side-only changes; quantify bias vs variance (DEFF) and cross-check against a holdout.

Related cases: G1 H3 S4

4. The result is significant but tiny. What do you do?

Compare the CI lower bound with the practical threshold (cost, maintenance, opportunity cost). Significance with n in the millions is cheap. Ship only if expected value is positive and the guardrails are flat.

Related cases: G1 G6

5. How do you separate short-term lift from long-term value?

Persistent holdout, track effect by weeks since exposure, test for decay, use validated surrogates for 28-90 day outcomes; do not extrapolate week-one lifts for engagement nudges.

Related cases: G7 H2

6. Stakeholder wants to stop the test at day 3, p = 0.03. Your response?

Explain the false-positive inflation from peeking; offer a pre-registered group-sequential or always-valid design so early stops are legitimate; keep a harm-only stop rule.

Related cases: G6

7. Randomization is impossible (policy change in one market). Approach?

DiD or synthetic control; prove pre-trends and donor-pool fit, run placebo tests, state the assumption leaders are buying, and give a range, not a point.

Related cases: G8 S6

8. How do you measure a support deflection or AI-resolution feature?

Primary: verified resolution (no re-contact in 7 days, no escalation). Guardrails: CSAT, repeat contact, safety escalation latency. Count contacts including repeats; a deflected contact that comes back is not a win.

Related cases: S2 S4

9. How do you prioritize an experimentation roadmap?

Expected value = P(win) × effect × reach × duration, divided by cost and time-to-learn; weight reversibility and strategic option value; reserve capacity for exploratory bets and platform fixes.

Related cases: H8 S3

10. A segment shows +8% but the overall effect is flat. Ship to the segment?

Only if the pre-registered interaction test supports it; otherwise it is a multiple-comparison artifact. Ship via a confirmatory test on that segment, with FDR control across cuts.

Related cases: G2 S1

11. How do you reduce variance without more traffic?

CUPED/regression adjustment, stratification, a better OEC (lower noise), triggered analysis on exposed units, and bounded or winsorized metrics for heavy tails.

Related cases: S7

12. How do you influence a PM who is committed to shipping?

Pre-register the decision rule together, frame the cost of a wrong decision in their metric, share a one-page readout with the range of outcomes, and propose the smallest follow-up that resolves the disagreement.

Related cases: H6 G5
